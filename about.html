My doctoral research focuses on understanding the neural mechanisms of movement preparation, voluntary action, and conscious intention, exploring how the brain transforms internally generated intentions into purposeful movements.

Research Interests and Objectives

  • Movement Preparation and Motor Planning: Investigating the neural mechanisms underlying the preparation, planning, and initiation of voluntary movements, particularly self-initiated actions.
  • Conscious Intention and Volitional Control: Exploring how conscious intentions emerge and interact with neural processes preceding voluntary movement.
  • Neural Correlates of Movement Preparation: Examining preparatory neural activity and corticospinal excitability to understand how the brain prepares the motor system for action.
  • Brain–Muscle Communication: Investigating the relationship between cortical activity, neural drive, and motor unit recruitment during movement preparation and execution using electromyography (EMG) and transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS).
  • Free Will and Intentional Binding: Studying the relationship between subjective intention, action initiation, and sensory consequences to understand the experience of voluntary action and perceived agency.
  • Motor Preparation and Conscious Awareness: Understanding the temporal relationship between subjective awareness of intention, preparatory neural processes, and the onset of voluntary movement.
  • Neural Mechanisms of Human Agency: Investigating how internally generated intentions are transformed into purposeful motor actions, contributing to our understanding of consciousness, self-agency, and voluntary motor control.
